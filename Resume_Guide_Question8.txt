# COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE TO PREPARING VARIOUS TYPES OF RESUMES
## Based on Technical and Professional Writing Standards
(Referencing principles from Blicq, R.S., Andrews, C.A., and Coryell, A.P.)

### 1. THE FUNDAMENTAL PHILOSOPHY: THE "PYRAMID" APPROACH
As per Blicq’s "Technically-Write!" methodology, a resume is not a history book—it is a marketing document. You do not list everything you have ever done; you present the most critical information first (the peak of the pyramid) and support it with evidence below.

---

### 2. TYPES OF RESUMES AND WHEN TO USE THEM

#### A. THE CHRONOLOGICAL RESUME (The Standard)
This is the most common format, focusing on a steady career progression.
- **Best For:** Candidates with a strong, continuous work history in a single field.
- **Structure:**
    - Contact Information
    - Professional Summary/Objective
    - Work Experience (Reverse Chronological: Most Recent $\rightarrow$ Oldest)
    - Education
    - Skills/Certifications
- **Key Rule:** Focus on *achievements* rather than *duties*. Instead of "Responsible for managing a team," use "Managed a team of 10 to increase productivity by 15%."

#### B. THE FUNCTIONAL RESUME (The Skill-Based)
This format de-emphasizes the timeline and highlights specific skill sets.
- **Best For:** Career changers, recent graduates with little experience, or those with significant employment gaps.
- **Structure:**
    - Contact Information
    - Professional Summary
    - **Core Competencies/Skills Clusters** (e.g., "Technical Project Management," "Software Development")
    - Brief Work History (Company, Title, Dates—no bullet points here)
    - Education
- **Key Rule:** Group your experience by *skill category* rather than by *employer*.

#### C. THE COMBINATION (HYBRID) RESUME
The most effective modern format, blending the best of both worlds.
- **Best For:** Highly skilled professionals who want to highlight specific expertise while still showing a solid work history.
- **Structure:**
    - Contact Information
    - Professional Summary
    - **Key Skills & Achievements** (Functional section)
    - **Professional Experience** (Chronological section)
    - Education
- **Key Rule:** Use the top section to "hook" the reader with your best wins, then use the bottom section to prove your stability.

---

### 3. TECHNICAL WRITING STANDARDS FOR RESUMES (The "Surgical" Detail)

Based on the standards of Andrews and Coryell, a technical resume must adhere to these strict guidelines:

#### I. Visual Hierarchy and Layout
- **White Space:** Use generous margins. A cluttered resume is a rejected resume.
- **Typography:** Use a professional sans-serif font (Arial, Calibri, Roboto). Avoid decorative fonts.
- **Consistency:** If you bold one job title, bold them all. If you use a period at the end of one bullet point, use them for all.

#### II. The Power of Action Verbs
Never start a sentence with "I was responsible for..." or "My duties included...". Use high-impact action verbs:
- **Instead of "Worked on":** $\rightarrow$ *Engineered, Developed, Orchestrated, Implemented.*
- **Instead of "Helped with":** $\rightarrow$ *Collaborated, Facilitated, Supported, Optimized.*
- **Instead of "Changed":** $\rightarrow$ *Transformed, Revitalized, Overhauled, Streamlined.*

#### III. Quantifiable Results (The Evidence)
A technical resume without numbers is just a list of opinions. 
- **Weak:** "Improved website speed."
- **Surgical:** "Reduced homepage load time from 4.2s to 1.8s, resulting in a 20% increase in user retention."

---

### 4. STEP-BY-STEP PREPARATION WORKFLOW

1. **Analyze the Target:** Read the job description. Identify the "Keywords." (e.g., if they want "Python" and "Agile," those words must appear in your summary and skill section).
2. **Draft the "Master List":** Write down every project, tool, and win you've ever had.
3. **Select the Format:** 
   - Linear path? $\rightarrow$ Chronological.
   - Switching fields? $\rightarrow$ Functional.
   - Expert status? $\rightarrow$ Combination.
4. **Surgical Edit:** Remove any fluff. Words like "hardworking," "team player," and "detail-oriented" are fillers. Replace them with *proof* of those traits.
5. **Final Review:** Check for "The Three S's": **S**pelling, **S**pacing, and **S**yntax. A single typo in a technical resume signals a lack of attention to detail.

---

### 5. SUMMARY CHECKS (The Final Audit)
- [ ] Is my contact info correct and professional?
- [ ] Does the summary clearly state who I am and what I bring to the table?
- [ ] Are my bullet points focused on *results* rather than *tasks*?
- [ ] Is the format consistent throughout the document?
- [ ] Is it saved as a PDF to preserve the layout?
